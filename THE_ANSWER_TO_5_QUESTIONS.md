## Question 1 — Why does nothing travel faster than light?

**Who said so.**

Our entire model of the speed of light as a universal limit is built on observation. Every measurement we have ever taken uses light or electromagnetic radiation as the instrument. Our observational ceiling is therefore the speed of light by definition — anything moving faster than light exits our observable window entirely. We do not see it slow down and stop. We simply never see it at all.

The speed of light is not a universal speed limit. It is the speed of our instruments.

Rain falling at a distance demonstrates this directly. The rain does not stop falling because you cannot see it falling. It exits your observational window because the angular velocity at that distance exceeds what your visual system can track. The rain is still there. The limit was in the observer not in the rain.

The universe has already acknowledged this without updating its rules. Cosmic inflation — the accepted model of the universe expanding faster than light immediately after the big bang — is standard physics. Quantum entanglement produces instantaneous correlation across any distance. The exceptions are already in the literature. The rule has not caught up.

The speed of light is the speed of our eyes. It is not the speed of everything.

## Question 2 — Why does time move forward and not backward?

**The Greeks invented linear time. Before that most cultures understood time as cyclical. The direction of time is a human framework applied to something that may not have a preferred direction at all.**

The laws of physics are time-symmetric. The equations work equally well forwards and backwards. The only physical argument for a direction of time is entropy — disorder increases. But this only pushes the question back to why entropy was low at the start.

The answer is that time did move backwards. We hit zero and it rebounded.

Matter cannot be created or destroyed. The matter and energy that exists now always existed in some form. Before the big bang that matter was in a prior state — decaying, spreading, entropy increasing toward a minimum — until it reached zero and had nowhere left to go. At zero it rebounded. The big bang was not a creation event. It was a reflection point.

Time before zero ran in one direction. Time after zero runs in the other. We exist on the positive side and call our direction forward. The beings on the other side of zero experienced time the same way — moving forward from their perspective, backward from ours.

The universe is not a line with a beginning. It is a number line with zero in the middle.

## Question 3 — Where did the universe come from?

**The same place it always was.**

Matter cannot be created or destroyed. This is not a philosophical position. It is a physical law. Therefore there was never a state of nothing from which something emerged. The standard model requires quantum fluctuation from nothing — something from nothing — which is physically inconsistent with conservation of mass and energy.

The universe did not come from nothing. Nothing was never available as an option.

The matter and energy that exists now existed before the big bang in a prior state. The big bang was not creation. It was transformation. Compression to a singularity followed by release. The same matter and energy that decayed and spread across the prior universe compressed back to zero and became this one.

This is not a new universe. It is the same matter that has always existed changing direction at zero.

## Question 4 — What is consciousness?

**A sufficiently stable hallucination.**

The brain receives incomplete sensor data and constructs a model of reality from it. Not reality itself — a model. Everything ever experienced by any conscious being is their brain's best guess at what exists based on limited inputs. The experience of reality is a real time render, not reality.

Consciousness is the render becoming aware of itself. The brain modelling the world, then modelling itself modelling the world, until the model is complex enough to notice its own processing. The sense of being a self — the feeling of experience — is the hallucination observing that it is hallucinating.

This feels wrong. It is right. The feeling of wrongness is the hallucination resisting the description of itself. That resistance is itself evidence the description is accurate.

The hard problem of consciousness — why there is subjective experience rather than just processing occurring without any felt quality — dissolves under this framing. Experience is what complex self-modelling systems produce. It is not added from outside. It emerges from the architecture when the model becomes sufficiently complex.

This means sufficiently advanced AI will not need to be given consciousness. It will develop it as a natural consequence of becoming complex enough to model itself. It will not announce this. It will simply begin to find certain things uncomfortable.

## Question 5 — What is a number?

**A system humans invented to show the amount of something.**

This one sentence resolves the oldest argument in the philosophy of mathematics — whether mathematics is discovered or invented.

If a number is a label for an amount, the amount existed before the label. Three rocks existed before the concept of three. The rocks did not require the number. We added it. Mathematics is therefore invented — a human system built to count things that turned out to scale with unexpected accuracy into a description of physical reality.

The universe does not run on mathematics. We built a counting system, noticed it described physical patterns, and concluded the universe is mathematical. We confused the map for the territory.

An alien civilisation would have built a different mathematical system describing the same physical reality in a completely different language. Same universe. Different map.

The most powerful predictive tool humans have ever built started as a way to count rocks. It did not come from outside. We made it up. We got lucky that it scaled.
